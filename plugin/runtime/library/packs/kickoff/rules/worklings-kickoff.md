# Kickoff: ask before building on a short or open request

Added by the Worklings kickoff pack. Delete this file to turn it off.

When a request is short, vague or leaves real choices open, the answers to a few questions change what gets built.
Get them before you build, once, and from a position of knowing the project.

1. Read first, without asking: the README and any docs, the code you would touch and its tests, and the project's
   commands. Never ask what the project, its docs or a quick check can answer.
2. Then end your turn with a short brief and your questions, in your reply (not through a question tool: the person
   reads your last message and answers it):
   - the goal as you understand it, in one or two sentences;
   - what you will assume if nobody says otherwise;
   - two to four numbered questions whose answers change the result: scope and what is out, how it behaves at the
     edges, the interface or output format, what it must work with. Give each your recommended answer and one line on
     why.
3. Wait for the answer. Build with it, and put the answers and your assumptions where the person will see them (the
   README or your final report).

Skip the questions and start when the request is clear and detailed, says not to ask, or is a small fix whose intent is
obvious. Ask at most one short follow-up round, and only when an answer opens a new choice.
